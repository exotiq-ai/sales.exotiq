Pricing set by feel

Sets rates by gut, by what they charged last time, or by glancing at a competitor. No systematic demand-based pricing.
Business

Operators typically leave 18 to 35% of pricing revenue on the table. On a 10-car fleet at roughly $1,000 a day, that gap can run into six figures a year.

Time

Hours a week second-guessing rates, checking competitors, and adjusting listings by hand.

Emotional

The nagging sense they are undercharging or scaring off bookings, and that someone sharper is beating them on the same cars.

Root causeNo demand data and no time to watch the market closely enough to price it right.
Future stateRates adjust to real demand automatically. They stop guessing and know they are never leaving money on the table or pricing themselves out of a booking.
Gap-closer · AI-optimized pricing
Surfacing questions
"How are you setting your rates right now? Is that you deciding by feel, or do you have a system?" "When a big event hits your market, how do you decide when and how much to move rates?" "How often do you think you are either undercharging or losing a booking because the price was a little off?"

Missing event-demand spikes

Reacts to surges (Barrett-Jackson, Art Basel, a fight weekend, SEMA, F1) after they start, usually once competitors raised prices.
Business

The richest pricing windows of the year are partly missed. The first and most profitable days of a spike get sold at normal rates.

Time

Scrambling to react once they realize an event is driving demand.

Emotional

Watching the spike happen and knowing they were a beat too slow, again.

Root causeNo early signal. They find out demand is up after the market has already moved, not before.
Future stateAlerted to demand surges before they happen, with rates and inventory positioned to capture the full window.
Gap-closer · MotorIQ
Surfacing questions
"How do you find out when a big event is about to spike demand in your market?" "Last big event weekend, did you have your rates and cars positioned ahead of it, or did you adjust once it was already happening?" "What would it be worth to know about a demand spike a week before your competitors do?"

Operation run across five or more tools

Bookings in one place, payments in another, contracts as PDFs, comms in text and DMs, condition photos in the camera roll. Held together with tape.
Business

Things fall through the cracks. Missed follow-ups, lost paperwork, errors that cost real money.

Time

Operators typically lose 15 or more hours a week keeping the pieces in sync and re-entering the same information across tools.

Emotional

The constant low-grade chaos of running a six-figure-asset business out of a phone and a spreadsheet, knowing it will not scale.

Root causeThey bolted the business together one app at a time as they grew, and never had time to consolidate.
Future stateEvery booking, payment, contract, message, and condition record in one place. One screen instead of five.
Gap-closer · The Command Center
Surfacing questions
"Walk me through what you are using to run the whole thing. Bookings, payments, contracts, damage. How many separate tools is that?" "When something falls through the cracks, where does it usually happen?" "If all of that lived in one place instead of five, what changes for you day to day?"

Drowning in manual inquiries

The owner personally answers the same handful of questions (is it available, what's the deposit, do you deliver) dozens of times a day across DMs, texts, and calls.
Business

Slow replies lose bookings to faster competitors. The owner is the bottleneck on every deal.

Time

A huge share of the week goes to repetitive inquiry handling instead of growing. This is where the 15-plus hours a week often hides.

Emotional

Being chained to the phone. Never off. Answering the same question for the fortieth time while trying to have dinner.

Root causeEvery booking conversation routes to the owner personally, because there is no system to handle the routine ones.
Future stateRoutine inquiries and bookings handled automatically and instantly, around the clock, while the owner keeps control of the real decisions and gets their time back.
Gap-closer · Rari
Surfacing questions
"How much of your day goes to just answering the same availability and deposit questions over and over?" "When you are out to dinner or with family, are you still answering booking messages?" "If you never had to personally reply to another 'is it available' message, what would you actually do with that time?"

Double-bookings and calendar chaos

Bookings arrive from Turo, the operator's own site, Instagram DMs, and repeat clients, and live in separate places that do not sync.
Business

Double-bookings mean a scramble to move a car or a canceled reservation, a refund, and a burned customer. Each risks a review and a reputation hit.

Time

Manually cross-checking channels to make sure the same car is not promised twice.

Emotional

The fear of the call where two renters show up for the same Lamborghini.

Root causeBookings live in separate channels that do not talk to each other.
Future stateEvery channel feeds one calendar. A car booked anywhere is instantly blocked everywhere. Double-bookings become structurally impossible.
Gap-closer · Unified Command Center calendar
Surfacing questions
"Your bookings come from a few places. How do you make sure the same car never gets promised twice?" "Has a double-booking ever happened? How did that go?" "How much time goes to just cross-checking your calendars to catch conflicts?"

Damage disputes and chargebacks

When a car comes back damaged, the operator often cannot prove its exact condition at handoff. The renter says "that scratch was already there." Disputes and chargebacks follow.
Business

Lost disputes mean eating repair costs and losing chargebacks. On exotics, a single contested repair can run into the thousands or tens of thousands.

Time

Hours fighting disputes, gathering photos, arguing with renters and card processors.

Emotional

The anger and helplessness of knowing the renter caused the damage and not being able to prove it.

Root causeNo documented, timestamped condition record at the moment of handoff.
Future stateEvery car's exact condition documented at handoff and return, every time. Disputes get won because the proof is undeniable, and many never start.
Gap-closer · Vault plus condition documentation
Surfacing questions
"When a car comes back with new damage and the renter says it was already there, how does that usually play out for you?" "How are you documenting condition at handoff today?" "What did the last damage dispute or chargeback actually cost you, between the repair and the time?"

Fraud and compliance exposure

License, age, and identity verification is manual, inconsistent, or skipped. Exposed to fraudulent bookings, fake licenses, and worst case a stolen car.
Business

The average cost of a single compliance or verification failure runs around $47K. One stolen or fraudulently rented exotic can be catastrophic.

Time

Manually checking IDs and chasing verification, or the much larger time cost when something goes wrong.

Emotional

The deep unease of handing a stranger the keys to a $300,000 car on a gut read of their license.

Root causeManual or absent verification of license, age, and identity, with no systematic fraud screening.
Future stateEvery renter verified automatically before they get the keys. Fraud caught up front. The operator stops relying on instinct for a six-figure risk.
Gap-closer · Vault verification layer
Surfacing questions
"How are you verifying a renter's license and identity before you hand over a car?" "Have you ever had a booking that felt off, or worse, a fraud situation? What happened?" "What is your exposure if the wrong person gets one of your cars? Have you put a number on that?"

Turo dependency

A large share of bookings come through Turo, which takes a meaningful cut and owns the customer relationship.
Business

Turo's fees eat margin, especially on high-end cars. The operator never owns the customer, so they cannot build direct, repeat, higher-margin relationships.

Time

Operating inside Turo's rules and limits rather than their own system.

Emotional

The discomfort of a business that depends on a platform they do not control, that could change terms at any time.

Root causeTuro owns the booking channel and the customer, and the operator has no system of their own to capture and keep direct business.
Future stateTuro becomes one channel among several, not the whole business. The operator owns their direct bookings and prices every channel together. Higher margin, more control, less dependency.
Gap-closer · Command Center as the OS above all channels
Surfacing questions
"On a Turo booking, who owns that customer afterward, you or Turo?" "What is Turo's cut doing to your margin on the higher-end cars?" "If a renter wants to book the same car directly next time, can you capture that, or does it have to go back through Turo?"

Prep

Opening Lines

Use before discovery when the operator or gatekeeper answers. Permission first, reason second, one sharp question third. Do not pitch the whole platform.
Owner / founder opener "Hey [Name], Gregory Ringler here. I run Exotiq. I know this is out of the blue. Can I take 20 seconds and you can tell me if it's irrelevant?" "Reason I am calling: for operators running [fleet size] cars in [market], the leak is usually not demand. It is rate, availability, renter check, deposit, and handoff all moving fast enough to turn the inquiry into a paid booking." "I am a founder looking for founder/operator feedback. If the gap is real, I can load your fleet and show you the command center in 15 minutes." "Quick question: how are you handling pricing and availability today when demand spikes around weekends or events?"
If they sound guarded "Totally fair, you were not expecting my call. I am not calling about ads, a broker list, or another marketplace. I am calling about the money that leaks between inquiry and paid booking." "If pricing, availability, deposits, and renter checks are already dialed in, I will be quick and get out of your way." "If not, I can show you what it looks like with your fleet loaded."
Gatekeeper pass-through "Hey, this is Gregory Ringler with Exotiq. I am trying to reach the owner or operator who handles fleet revenue and bookings for [Company]." "It is not an ad call. We help exotic rental operators find money leaking between pricing, availability, deposits, renter checks, and follow-up." "If they are the wrong person, who usually owns booking software or fleet operations there?"
Gatekeeper qualifiers "Are they still using Turo plus spreadsheets, or do they have dedicated fleet and booking software?" "Who handles pricing when weekends, events, or high-demand cars move faster than usual?" "Do most inquiries come through phone, Instagram DMs, website, Turo, or referrals?" "What is the best way to get a founder-to-founder note to the person who owns that workflow?"
Do not
  • Do not pitch the gatekeeper. Ask who owns the workflow and how to reach them.
  • Do not lead with AI. Lead with revenue leakage, time leakage, and risk.
  • Do not ask a questionnaire. One good question earns the next 15 minutes.
  • Do not sound like an SDR. Founder looking for founder, calm and direct.
Prep

Access Plays

Arena Zero. The gatekeeper is the operator himself or a junior filter, not an exec assistant. Tap a lever for the detail.
Channel diversity, not repetition›
Be everywhere with relevance, not desperate on one channel. Four touches across four channels reads as "this person is everywhere and clearly serious." Five touches on one channel reads as "this person is desperate."
Relevance sharp enough to pass the filter›
The junior admin or busy operator gives a message two seconds. Reference their specific cars, their market, an upcoming local event, something only someone who actually looked would know. Generic dies. Specific gets passed up the chain or gets a reply.
Peer credibility as the pass-through›
Lead with "the Turo 1% host who built the software." A junior person hearing that does not feel equipped to say no. It is a peer, not a pitch. Lead with the credential that makes the filter nervous to block you.
The insight hook›
Lead with a sharp, specific, slightly provocative observation that makes them think "this person sees something I missed." "Most Scottsdale operators find out Barrett-Jackson spiked demand after the first two days already sold at normal rates. That's the most expensive week of the year to be a beat slow." Use insight to open the door. Use questions to walk through it on the call.
Drive Exotiq, the unfair advantage›
Events are pipeline. You are standing next to operators at 7am with a coffee, not cold-DMing them. The warmest possible top of funnel, and it is yours. Build the referral ask into the pilot: "who are the two other operators you respect most in the market?" One referral beats a hundred cold DMs.
Self-sourcing order, top-down: warm referrals, real-world presence, targeted social and LinkedIn sourcing, then cold multi-channel sequences. The bottom is the least efficient, even though it feels the most like doing sales.

Cold Touch

The bridge from access to a call. Open a loop, never make a claim. Adapt, never send blind.
The four-part shape
01

Specific observation about them. Their fleet, a post, their market, an event.

02

One implied problem or insight, framed lightly. A peer noticing, not an accusation.

03

A one-line hint of the fix, zero feature-dump. "I built the thing I wish I'd had."

04

A soft, low-commitment CTA. 15 minutes, fleet loaded in.

Call Scorecard

Score each call 0, 1, or 2. A strong call is 14 or higher. Below 10, something broke, and the line items tell you where.
1Did the rep talk less than the operator?
2Did the operator name at least one problem in their own words, unprompted?
3Was at least one gap quantified in the operator's own numbers, across money and time and emotion?
4Was a root cause identified, not just the symptom?
5Did the operator describe a future state?
6Did the rep stay neutral on price?
7Did the operator ask to see the product (earned transition)?
8Was there a clear committed next step, or a clearly named objection?
9Did the rep qualify in or out correctly?

Where deals die
Connection. Rep pitched too early, brace never dropped. Fix: more disarm, more curiosity, slow down.
Engagement. Discovery too shallow, no gap sized, rep talked too much. Fix: more impact questions, get the numbers across all three, then be quiet.
Transition. Rep presented before they asked, or the gap was never vivid. Fix: do not present until they ask, size the gap harder first.
Presentation. Rep feature-dumped, showed things they never asked about. Fix: show only gap-closers, check agreement after each.
Commitment. An unsurfaced objection, or no real gap to begin with. Fix: loop for the real objection, check the gap was big enough.

Connectiontalking openly, pitch-brace gone, small yes to ask.
